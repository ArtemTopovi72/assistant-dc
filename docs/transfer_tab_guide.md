# How to Use the Transfer Tab

## What the Transfer tab is

The **Transfer** tab lets you take something from one picture and place it onto another — for example, putting a jacket, a hat, or a hairstyle from one photo onto a person in a different photo. The person's face and identity stay the same; only the thing you asked to transfer changes.

You'll find it on the right-hand panel, next to the **Images** tab.

Use it when you have two (or more) pictures and want to combine them — the "where it comes from" picture and the "where it goes" picture.

---

## Step 1 — Add your images

You need **at least two images**:

- **Source** — the picture that *contains the object* you want to copy (the jacket, hat, etc.).
- **Target** — the picture you want to *change* (usually the person).
- **(Optional) Extra reference** — an additional picture for style or mood.

To add them:

1. Click **➕ Add images…** and pick the pictures from your computer.
2. *Or* click **⤵ Use loaded** to pull in every image you've already pasted or opened during this session.

Each picture you add shows up as a row with a small thumbnail and its file name.

> Tip: Click **✕ Clear** at any time to remove everything and start over.

---

## Step 2 — Assign roles

Every image needs a **role**, chosen from the drop-down menu on its row.

The two roles you'll use most:

- **Target (edit this)** → *where the object will be placed* (the picture that gets changed).
- A **source** role → *where the object comes from*. Pick the one that fits:
  - **Clothing source** – for jackets, dresses, shirts, etc.
  - **Object source** – for hats, glasses, props, accessories.
  - **Hairstyle reference** – for hair.
  - **Face reference** – to apply a face.

Simple way to remember it:

> **Source = where the object comes from.**
> **Target = where the object will be placed.**

Optional roles, if you want a particular look rather than a specific object:

- **Style reference** – copy the overall look/colors.
- **Scene reference** / **Lighting reference** – borrow a background or mood.

A few rules the tab handles for you:

- Only **one** image can be the Target. If you mark a new one as Target, the previous one is automatically switched back.
- The newest image you add is set as the Target by default — change it if that's not what you want.
- If you're not sure which role to pick, leave it on **Auto-infer role** and the app will guess based on your instruction.

Then type a short instruction in the text box at the bottom, for example:

> *"put the jacket from the reference on the person"*

---

## Step 3 — Run the transfer

You have three buttons:

- **👁 Preview plan** – *(optional)* shows what the app intends to do and previews the cut-out object, **without** changing your image yet. Good for a quick sanity check.
- **▶ Run contained transfer** – **the one you normally want.** It changes only the relevant area and keeps the person's face and identity intact.
- **⚠ Run whole-frame** – a faster, rougher option that redraws the whole picture. It can slightly change the face, so use it only if the contained version isn't giving you what you need.

After you click **Run contained transfer**:

1. The status line shows progress messages (preparing, transferring, etc.).
2. Processing usually takes **about a minute** — sometimes a little more for large images. This is normal.
3. When it finishes, the result appears automatically.

> Leave the app running while it works — don't close the tab mid-transfer.

---

## Step 4 — Understand the results

When the transfer is done, the four preview boxes at the bottom fill in:

- **Target** – your original picture (the "before").
- **Extracted asset** – the object the app cut out of the source picture (the jacket, hat, etc.), shown on a transparent background. *This only appears for object/clothing-type transfers.*
- **Target region mask** – highlights the area on the target that was changed.
- **Output** – the finished picture (the "after").

Comparing the **Target** box (before) with the **Output** box (after) gives you an instant before/after view.

Other things that happen automatically:

- The finished image is added to the **Images** tab so you can browse it full-size.
- **Double-click** any preview box to open that picture at full size.
- A short note confirms the result was saved, along with a quality check confirming the person's identity was preserved.

---

## Common mistakes & tips

1. **Only one image loaded.** A transfer always needs at least two — a source *and* a target. If you only add one, the app will ask for another.

2. **Source and Target swapped.** If the result changed the wrong picture, you probably assigned the roles backwards. Remember: **Source** is where the object *comes from*, **Target** is the picture that *gets changed*.

3. **Unclear object in the source picture.** The app works best when the object you want is clearly visible and not hidden behind other things. A clean, well-lit source photo where the jacket/hat/etc. is easy to see gives much better results than a cluttered or blurry one.

4. **Too many roles at once.** Start simple — one source and one target. Add extra references only once the basic transfer works.

5. **Picked the wrong source role.** Use **Clothing source** for garments and **Object source** for accessories like hats and glasses. If you're unsure, leave it on **Auto-infer role** and write a clear instruction.

6. **Closed the tab too early.** Give it the full minute or so to finish — switching away or clearing while it's running cancels the work.

---

### Quick recap

1. **➕ Add images…** (at least two)
2. Set one as **Target**, the other as a **source** role
3. Type what to transfer
4. Click **▶ Run contained transfer**
5. Wait ~1 minute, then compare **Target** vs **Output**
